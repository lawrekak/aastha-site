First full category-scored audit (the 4 October note was a tooling baseline without category scores). Scored by hand from `.wip/geo-data/` (collected 2026-10-13, no Claude), `audit-report.md` and a few direct checks of `dist/`, per `.github/seo-agent.md` and `.claude/skills/geo/RULE36.md`. `geo-audit`'s own subagents were not run.

## Composite GEO score: 73/100 — Fair

The `geo-audit` skill weights AI Citability 25%, Brand Authority 20%, Content E-E-A-T 20%, Technical 15%, Schema 10%, Platform 10%. This site does not score Brand Authority (brand mentions are audit-only under Rule 36 — nothing is built to move that number), so the other five weights are renormalised to sum to 100%:

| Category | Weight (renormalised) | Score |
|---|---:|---:|
| AI visibility (citability, crawlers, llms.txt) | 31.25% | 70/100 |
| Content & E-E-A-T | 25% | 62/100 |
| Technical | 18.75% | 88/100 |
| Schema & structured data | 12.5% | 85/100 |

`0.3125×70 + 0.25×62 + 0.1875×88 + 0.125×85 + 0.125×68 = 73.0`

## AI visibility — 70/100
- **Crawler access: 100/100.** `robots.txt` explicitly allows every major AI crawler (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Bingbot, Googlebot, DuckAssistBot, meta-externalagent, CCBot) and declares the sitemap.
- **llms.txt: 100/100.** Valid, 5 sections, served as UTF-8 text alongside `llms-full.txt`.
- **Citability: ≈39/100 average across the 17 sampled pages**, driving this category down. Blog posts average ≈42 (the land-law post was 42.8 at baseline); practice-area pages average ≈36 and are *meant* to score low — their explanatory text was deliberately removed in September 2026 (RULE36.md). The gap is not a bug, but it does mean practice pages still carry little passage-level citability.

## Content & E-E-A-T — 62/100
- Strong authorship signals: `Person` schema carries `hasCredential` (the Bar Council enrolment and LL.B.), `alumniOf`, `memberOf`, `knowsAbout`, and an `author` meta tag on every page.
- Blog posts (13 so far) are frequent, dated, cite primary sources (`citation` in `BlogPosting`), and lead with an answer-first passage per the `daily-law-blog` skill's rule.
- Held back by: `Person.image` is still the generic site OG image, not a real photograph — a real, recognisable photo is one of the stronger E-E-A-T/trust signals for a solo advocate, and AI Overviews and the Knowledge Panel favour it. Practice-area pages are intentionally thin on depth (by design, see above), which also caps this score on a strict E-E-A-T reading.

## Technical — 88/100
- `scripts/audit-live.mjs` found nothing needing attention on the live site.
- Security headers present on every page sampled: HSTS, `permissions-policy`, `referrer-policy`, `x-content-type-options: nosniff`, `x-frame-options`.
- Fully server-rendered, valid sitemap (35 URLs), valid robots.txt.
- Not scored higher because PageSpeed Insights returned HTTP 429 this run — Core Web Vitals are unverified, not confirmed bad.

## Schema & structured data — 85/100
- Comprehensive graph: WebSite, LegalService/LocalBusiness, Person, BreadcrumbList, WebPage, Service, FAQPage, BlogPosting (with `citation` and author) — unchanged from and consistent with the 4 October baseline.
- No Review/AggregateRating anywhere, which is correct under Rule 36, not a gap.
- Real gap: `LegalService.geo` (GeoCoordinates) is absent because `content/site.json`'s `geo` field is still `null` — nobody has supplied the Google Business Profile pin's lat/lng yet.

## Platform readiness — 68/100
- Favourable for Google AI Overviews and Bing Copilot: FAQPage schema, server-side rendering, Google-Extended and Bingbot both explicitly allowed.
- Favourable for Perplexity and ChatGPT web search: `llms.txt`/`llms-full.txt`, sourced blog posts with named courts/sections/dates that read as self-contained, quotable answers.
- Held back for all platforms by the same low citability on practice-area and top-level pages noted above, and by a single-profile `sameAs` (LinkedIn only) which gives entity-resolution systems (Gemini/Google in particular) less to anchor to than a fuller set of verified profiles would.
